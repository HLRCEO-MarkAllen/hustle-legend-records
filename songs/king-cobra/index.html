10 · BATTLE RAP AS BOUNDARY

King Cobra

The insults are the fireworks. Underneath them is an argument about ownership, respect and who gets to define the artist.

Behind the conflict

“King Cobra” is a diss record, but its place on the album makes it more than a standalone attack. It arrives after “Loser,” where outside judgment is already under examination. Here that pressure becomes direct confrontation.

The deeper tension is about value: whether another person gets to diminish the work, the history or the artist behind it. Battle-rap aggression becomes a way of drawing a boundary.

Why it matters

Born 2 Be A Legend does not clean up every ugly emotion. This track leaves conflict visible. That matters because the album is built as autobiography, not reputation management.
